# ADR-0086 — audit note

*Verified 2026-08-28. ADR-level verdict: `complete`. Written during this ADR's
de-amendment rewrite; it holds the measurements, the build history and the
scaffolding the folded document drops.*

## What the fold removed, and why

**The `## What is current` table.** A six-row "where the live answer is" index
mapping each topic to the amendment that last touched it. Pure fold scaffolding:
it existed because six amendments made the document unreadable front-to-back.
A single-voice ADR has one live answer per topic by construction, so the table
would restate its own decision list.

**The "a citation naming *ADR-0086's reversal amendment* is ambiguous" note.**
Two different amendments could be called that — Amendment 4 reversed the
`channel_mask` direction, Amendment 6 reversed Amendment 5's decision 2. The
ambiguity is an artifact of nickname citation; it dies once every citer names a
`dec. N`, which this commit does. (This is the "reversal amendment" hazard
recorded corpus-wide in the earlier audit pass.)

**Six amendment banners, six `### Decisions (author-ratified)` headings, three
`### Build note` sections, and the per-amendment `**Status:**` lines.** The
build notes' *rules* survive inside the decisions they constrain (decision 6's
"the address prints the token the id carries", decision 16's "both hit-test
passes", decision 26's deep-copy-on-restore); their narrative — who found it,
which fixture misled them, what the previous fix's shadow was — is here.

**Amendment 3's `superseded on reach by ADR-0095` block.** Already stale when
the rewrite began: ADR-0095's two-sided grip band was built and then **removed**
when this branch merged down. The folded ADR records it as one clause in
`## Considered and rejected` and keeps ADR-0095 in the Status line's supersedes
list.

## Numbering

The base `## Decision` was **unnumbered bullets**; every amendment's decisions
sat under `###` headings, which `check_adr_anchors.py`'s `anchors_of()` does not
register as decision anchors (`in_decision` flips only on a `##` heading). So
ADR-0086 published **zero** `dec. N` anchors before this commit and no
`ADR-0086 dec. N` citation existed anywhere in the tree. Fresh 1–26 numbering
was therefore free — the append-only rule had nothing to preserve.

Provenance: decisions 1–4 are the base `## Decision`; 5–6 Amendment 1 (#286,
plus its build note); 7–10 Amendment 2 (#287-adjacent); 11–14 Amendment 3;
15–21 Amendment 4; 22–25 Amendments 5–6 merged (the identity rule as reversed);
26 Amendment 6's decision 3.

## The correction this rewrite makes

Amendment 6's `**Status:**` line and the `## What is current` table both said
**"Decision 3 (restore-then-reapply) is NOT yet built."** It is built. The tree:
`EffectEditSession.gd` declares `var _coalesce_snapshot: Dictionary`, and
`apply_edit` calls `_restore_coalesce_pristine()` when `_coalesce_active and not
_coalesce_snapshot.is_empty() and _ref_key(field_ref) == _coalesce_key` — the
bracket-key gate decision 26 requires. `tests/ColourDragPristineTest.gd` is its
guard. Recorded `built`, not designed.

## Measurements worth keeping

- **Spacers.** Across all 481 effects, **1522 of 5304 camera sub-channel spans
  (28.7%)** are spacers; 58 lanes (2.7%) go fully empty, nearly all
  `for_each/zoom` on E001–E014. E317's for-each — the motivating case — resolves
  to `CASTER → ⟨hold⟩ → TARGET → ⟨hold⟩ → SLOT_COPY` on angle and position, and
  `DIRECT → ⟨hold⟩ → SLOT_COPY` on zoom.
- **The blind-boundary suppression, measured across 401 effects:** it cost
  camera 56 boundaries (1.1%) and colour 1945 (12–17%) — ~2001 suppressed
  against the ~1992 it re-attributed. That is what retired Amendment 5's
  decision 2, one day after it shipped in `0da2a5e18`.
- `EffectScoreModel` emits exactly `camera`, `camera_compiled`, `palette`,
  `screen`, `particle`, `sound` — **no** `palette_compiled`, **no**
  `screen_compiled`. This is the fact the suppression's justification assumed
  away.

## Three failure modes this area produced

1. **Acting on a predicate's consumer list rather than the predicate.**
   `is_hidden_spacer` was correct throughout; only one of its two hit-test
   consumers asked it, so testing the predicate proved nothing (decision 16).
2. **Confusing "is this drawn?" with "who owns this affordance."** The grip list
   never consulted the drawn-verdict, deliberately (decisions 18, 22).
3. **Over-correction — scoping a fix by reasoning about topology from one
   fixture, then firing it on 5,000+ spans.** The rule was right about camera,
   where the author was looking, and wrong about colour by a factor of fifteen.
   The procedural lesson: *measure the corpus before changing a rule that fires
   on every span, and measure it per lane kind when the kinds decide the
   predicate differently.*

## Guards

No `tools/check_*.py` guard names this ADR; 22 GDScript suites do, and the
folded `## Verification` names them. The decisions that would most repay a static
guard are the cross-kind invariants — that `edge_grip_identity` is the only
answer to grip ownership, and that no lane kind other than particle stamps a
grip rule of its own.

## Citation surface at fold time

40 files cite ADR-0086. Thirteen citations were anchor-checked; nine of those
were bare `ADR-0086 amendment`, which `check_adr_anchors.py` resolves only via
`HAS_AMD` — i.e. only while the word "amendment" survives somewhere in the file.
A clean fold removes it, so every one had to be retargeted to a `dec. N` in this
same commit. The rest are unanchored nicknames ("the boundary-drag amendment",
"the second amendment", "the reversal amendment") across
`src/effects/studio/*.gd`, `tests/*.gd`, in-corpus references in ADR-0071,
ADR-0087, ADR-0095, ADR-0101, and `docs/context/16-effect-studio-authoring-tool.md`.
